# Concept: The Why Behind Key Opens — Nq Cam

**Source:** "Time Shapes the Story Price Tells | The Why Behind Key Opens" — Nq Cam — https://youtu.be/afLme-akOwE
**Plugs into:** Step 4 (PO3 / time) of `01-ifvg-model.md`. Goes deeper than `02-key-opening-prices.md`; pairs with `14-stacked-po3.md`.

> **Core idea:** a key open is only useful once you know **what that candle's PO3 has already done** and **what it's still missing**.
> Opens are used two ways: as **continuation reference points** and as **draws on liquidity**.

---

## The 3 questions for every key open
1. **What has this PO3 done so far?** (opened / manipulated / distributed?)
2. **What is still unfinished?** (missing manipulation? distribution not done?)
3. **Where is price likely to deliver next** to finish it?

---

## Use 1: continuation reference point
- **When:** the HTF candle (e.g. **6:00 = 4H open**) has **opened → manipulated → started distributing**, but its DOL is **still intact**.
- **Then:** on lower-TF PO3s (9:30, 9:45, 10:00…), look for a **retrace back to that open**. It's the time EQ and the defense for the continuation, ideally with a price premium/discount, a PDA and a stop hunt.
- ❌ If the candle **never manipulated** on the other side of its open, don't use its open for continuation. That manipulation is still missing.

## Use 2: draw on liquidity
- **When:** a candle opened and **never manipulated on one side** of its open, e.g. a 4H candle went straight down to sell-side with no wick above its open.
- **Then:** that open is a **target**. Expect a later PO3 to go back above it to supply the missing manipulation.
- He rarely targets opens otherwise.

---

## Examples (summarised)
1. **6:00 4H went straight down** (no wick above its open) → 9:30 took major sell-side = HTF manipulation → **9:45 opened, swept lower (stop hunt) and turned up**, leaving a **rejection block** → 10:00 retraced exactly into **9:45's open + rejection block** + ES bullish SMT → continued higher toward **6:00's open** (the missing manipulation).
2. **Short (his trade):** 6:00 swept liquidity inside a bearish PDA and started lower, with sell-side still intact → at the open price rallied into **6:00's open** + bearish PDA in premium, then a stop hunt above it → **9:45 opened and wicked up** into a rejection block at 6:00's open → down to the DOL.
3. **Monthly open as DOL:** the month opened and ran up with no manipulation below its open → the market turned bearish → **6:00 opened, manipulated up, started down** → price rallied to 6:00's open (time EQ + premium) = high of the session → ran down below the monthly open.
4. **True day open (midnight):** opened with no manipulation below it and ran up, so the daily top wick is likely in → many DOLs below → price used the true day open short-term to rebalance 6:00's PO3, then continued lower.
5. **Stacked opens:** midnight + 6:00 both manipulated up, DOLs below intact; 8:30 added liquidity below → **9:30 rallied into the true day open + 6:00 open to the tick** (bearish PDA, CE of 6:00's range, premium) → continued lower.

## Daily routine
- [ ] Mark midnight, 6:00, 8:30, 9:30, 9:45, 10:00 (plus weekly/monthly when relevant)
- [ ] For each: done / missing (manipulation? distribution?)
- [ ] Opens with **missing manipulation** → potential DOL
- [ ] Opens that **manipulated + DOL still intact** → continuation reference
- [ ] Wait for an LTF PO3 to retrace to that open with a PDA + stop hunt → IFVG
- ⚠️ Don't stack 20 indicators you barely understand. **Backtest this on random days first.**
