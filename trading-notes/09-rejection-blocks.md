**Source:** "Understanding Rejection Blocks" — Nq Cam — https://youtu.be/lDN_RTJshSk

---

## How to mark it
| Type | Zone |
|---|---|
| **Bearish rejection block** | **Highest open/close (body) → highest wick** of the swing high |
| **Bullish rejection block** | **Lowest open/close (body) → lowest wick** of the swing low |

- **Mean threshold / CE** = 50% of the block. Respecting it = strength. Quadrants/octants give finer levels inside it.
- **Why it works:** liquidity rests just beyond candle **bodies**; price doesn't need to take the wick extreme if that extreme is truly **high resistance**. Wicks are inefficiency, so a revisit of the wick is just hitting another PDA in the trend direction.

---

## A rejection block is only VALID if
- [ ] The swing that formed it **swept significant liquidity** (ideally **inside an HTF PDA**) → it's **high resistance (HR)**
- [ ] **Displacement away** after it formed (not just stalling), creating PDAs in the new direction
- [ ] It's **recent / in close proximity** (old ones become swing points that get fully swept)
- [ ] It fits your **narrative / order flow** (don't trade against bias)
- [ ] You can **name the liquidity it took**

**Extra edge (his findings):** **exhaustion / overextension** before the block forms — a big run that used up its fuel and left unmitigated PDAs behind.

---

## Entries
- **Limit orders on rejection blocks = "mid"** — prefer waiting for a **reaction/displacement** away from it.
- The reaction is fast → be ready on **5s–30s** charts for an IFVG (or CISD). Waiting for a big HTF inversion = bad entry.
- Price can **revisit a block several times** (even re-sweeping inside it) while **holding the mean threshold** before the real move — expect possible break-evens.

## Targets
- **Reversal off a rejection block** → target **unmitigated PDAs** (low-hanging fruit). Price often uses the block for a short-term objective, then comes back to take the true wick extreme.
- **Continuation** → target **swings / liquidity** (unmitigated PDAs also fine).

## Example contexts from the video
1. **Before 10:00, 6am set the dealing range** → price rallied into **premium of that range + 6am open (time premium)**, swept equal highs inside a 4m BPR → instant displacement lower → limit at the block's CE → full TP to sell-side.
2. **Reversal:** big sell-off took a 4H intermediate low inside a 4H bullish FVG (HTF manipulation) → stop hunt → generated targets above → bullish rejection block in heavy time + price discount → rally to DOLs.
3. **Asia session:** overextended pump into bearish PDAs (other rejection blocks), many unmitigated bullish PDAs below → bearish rejection block (high swept inside a 5m BPR), wicks stayed in its lower half → several entries, finally ran to the unmitigated 1H gap. Lower-timeframe sell model inside a higher-timeframe buy model.
4. **Weekly 30m blocks** got reused many times. Common pattern: **NQ taps the block body while ES takes the wick extreme** → SMT. Both still count as "in distribution" (NQ took liquidity resting beyond the body).

## Common mistakes
- ❌ Using huge wicks as rejection blocks (they tend to get filled)
- ❌ Calling every swing high/low a rejection block
- ❌ Blocks that defend **low resistance**, or that are actually a major swing meant to be taken
- ❌ No displacement / intent after the block formed
- ❌ Trading against order flow / narrative
- ❌ Skipping the stop hunt — a good reversal still needs **major sweep → stop hunt aligned with time**; the rejection block becomes relevant **after** the stop hunt
- ❌ Using a concept live before backtesting it
